# Wen — Round 2

Marketing data analyst, light fit: occasional headshot on neutral/brand background for a
report cover or wiki. Default today is bugging a designer in Slack or fighting GIMP. Still
skeptical of any tool that transforms my image invisibly.

## Re-check of my Round-1 complaints
1. **Naming collision — FIXED.** Up by the photo it now reads "Remove cast shadow — Cleans
   the dark shadow under your subject"; the export toggle is plainly "Drop shadow." Two
   distinct names, and the "Remove cast shadow" one carries a clarifying tail. On first read
   I no longer worried that "Drop shadow" would re-add what "Remove" stripped — they read as
   opposite, clearly-scoped things.
2. **Drop shadow below the fold — FIXED.** It now sits on the SAME row as Background (White/
   Color/Transparent) and the Margin slider. Measured: with the Export panel in view,
   Background label, Margin, and Drop shadow are all visible together (Margin and Drop shadow
   share a row ~250px above the fold). I'd no longer skim past it.
3. **Edge fringe** — unchanged, as expected (free in-browser model limit). Not a regression;
   I'm not re-counting it against the score beyond the standing nit.

Nice bonus I hadn't asked for: with Drop shadow ON the download button relabels to
"Download white JPEG (with drop shadow)". As a data-hygiene person I like that the export
names exactly what's baked in — no hidden transform.

## 1. CLARITY — Yes
Unchanged from R1 and still strong. H1 "Remove any background — keep your photo on this
device", the "free, no upload, no signup" subhead, the three captioned preview tiles, and
the green "Your photos never leave this device" badge told me what it is and who it's for in
well under 30s. ~50MB model + cutout completed in ~25s in my test, no surprises.

## 2. VALUE — Yes
Still beats my alternatives for the once-a-month job: clean head/shoulders cutout, true
white (not sneaky gray), size presets that save a downstream resize, all local with no
account. The fixes make the drop-shadow effect (the Photoroom paid look) actually
discoverable now, which adds value I'd previously have missed entirely.

## 3. ADVOCACY — 9/10 (R1 was 8)
Both things I flagged are genuinely resolved, and the button-relabel earns extra trust, so
I'm moving up. Not a 10 only because for ME it stays a light-fit, ~monthly tool — I won't
evangelize it weekly the way a daily user would — and the faint edge fringe on
high-contrast backgrounds is still the one cosmetic thing I'd warn a friend about. But I'd
now recommend it without the "watch out for the confusing shadow controls" caveat I'd have
attached last round.
