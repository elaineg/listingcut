# Tomás — Round 1

Operations analyst, mid-size company. Corporate Windows/Edge, IT blocks installs, Excel/Tableau/Jira/Teams/SharePoint. I'd use this for the occasional product shot or headshot on a clean/branded background for a deck or SharePoint header — but I will NOT feed company imagery to a site that uploads it.

## 1. CLARITY — Yes
In ~10 seconds I knew exactly what it is and who it's for. The h1 "Remove any background — keep your photo on this device" does double duty: function + the privacy hook I care about most. The subline "free, no upload, no signup" and the use-case row ("Headshots & avatars · ads & social · slides & mockups · marketplace listings") told me a slide deck is a supported use case. The three White/Color/Transparent example tiles made the output formats obvious without reading anything.

## 2. VALUE — Yes
Today I either (a) bug a designer over Teams and wait a day, or (b) fight with PowerPoint's "Remove Background" tool, which is clumsy on anything with a busy backdrop. This was faster and cleaner than PowerPoint, and it's a browser tool so IT's install ban is a non-issue. The "Slide 16:9 (1920×1080)" export preset is exactly my deck dimension — I didn't have to resize afterward. The Margin slider and per-marketplace presets are bonus. Cutout on my test image was clean and the export downloaded a proper 1080×1080 JPEG with one click.
The Shadow toggle (new) is genuinely useful for slides — a product on a flat white panel looks pasted-on; the soft shadow makes it sit on the page. I found it easily under "Export background," it works on both White and Color, the preview updates live, and the download button even relabels to "Download JPEG on this background (with shadow)" so I know what I'm getting. Photoroom paywalls this; here it's free.

## 3. ADVOCACY — 8/10
I'd recommend it to coworkers, and the privacy angle ("photos never leave this device," and I confirmed in my own check that nothing was uploaded over the network) is exactly the line that gets a wary corporate user past the "is this safe?" hesitation. Held back from 9–10:
- The privacy claim is the whole reason I'd trust it with company imagery, but it's still just a claim on screen. A skeptical IT reviewer would want a one-line "how it works / verify offline" note (e.g., "disconnect your wifi — it still works"). That's what turns me from a quiet user into someone who forwards it to the team.
- First-photo "~50 MB model" download on a throttled corporate network could be a multi-minute stall; the progress bar helps but I'd want a clearer time estimate.

## What worked
- Privacy promise is front-and-center and, in my network check, true (no upload requests fired).
- Slide 16:9 preset + Shadow = a deck-ready product shot in one screen.
- Shadow is findable, tasteful (not a harsh halo), and clearly labeled in the download CTA.

## What held me back
- "Trust me" privacy still needs a verifiable proof point for corporate users.
- Model-download wait could bite on a slow work network with no ETA.
