**Clarity — Yes.** Within 5s I knew exactly what it is: a free, in-browser background remover. The H1 "Remove any background — keep your photo on this device" plus the subhead "free, no upload, no signup" nailed it, and the three preview tiles (White / Color / Transparent) showed the output formats without me reading further. The "Private by design — turn on airplane mode, it still works / open the Network tab — zero image uploads" line is exactly the kind of claim I'd actually verify in devtools, and it lands as a trust flex rather than marketing fluff. **Value — Yes.** Today my options are: fire up remove.bg (which paywalls full-res/PNG and nags for signup), or hand-mask in Figma/Photoshop, or wire up a `rembg` python script for a one-off placeholder — all annoying for a 30-second task. Here I clicked "try the sample," waited for the one-time model download, and got a clean cutout with an instant client-side export. For a README/Storybook screenshot or a demo asset this genuinely saves me real friction vs. any of those. The 20-image batch + ZIP is a nice bonus I didn't even need but would use.

The download button relabeled itself "Download JPEG on this gradient — 1080×1080 (Square)" and a left-side live preview updated immediately — good feedback. I exported and verified the file: real 1080×1080 RGB JPEG (no alpha), gradient genuinely baked in, and the mug edges are crisp against the blue — no white halo or matte fringe. This is the feature that gets me to drop the link in team Slack: branded backgrounds without opening Figma.

Console: 0 errors across the whole flow. Only nit: I tested on the bundled sample mug (a clean synthetic asset), so edge quality there is easy mode — I'd want to confirm hairline edges on a real photo before fully trusting it, but the compositing pipeline itself is solid.
